Ten checks before a file is locked

Doesn't 97.5% free allocation make 2026 almost free?

No. The factor scales the 1.53 t benchmark, not the exporter's emissions. A bolt on India's default carries 6.29 t, so 4.80 certificates a tonne remain.

Why can't an exporter verify only its own plant?

It can, but the wire rod then sits on India's default of about 4.3 t plus mark-up. That still costs about ₹19,300 a tonne more than using the mill's report.

Why would a mill share its data?

It already prepares the report for its own EU customers. Sharing is free, prices stay masked, and traced steel is what EU buyers ask for.

What if a stockist mixes lots?

The ledger caps each lot at the tonnes bought. Mixed or untraced tonnes take the default for their share only, so the rest of the file still counts.

Only five verifiers are accredited. Is there capacity?

Slots are booked in December 2026 with two accredited bodies; their Indian branches do field work, and one cluster campaign covers about ten sites a week.

Who is liable for a wrong file?

The accredited verifier signs; Carbon Khata acts as data processor with capped liability and professional indemnity cover.
